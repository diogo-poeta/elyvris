How it works and model limits

  1. Dataset selection

    Optical constants depend on deposition, temperature and crystal structure. Each dataset includes the reported sample conditions and reference. The ALD TiO₂ record is not a measurement of my own films.

  2. Polarization

    At normal incidence, s and p have the same power response. At oblique incidence, their responses generally differ. Unpolarized illumination averages the two powers.

  3. Data provenance

    CSV and tab-separated TXT exports include spectral results, settings and the selected material references. No values are extrapolated beyond a record’s supported range.

METHOD & SCOPE

Optical calculation and references

A single isotropic film, with wavelength-dependent n + ik, on a semi-infinite substrate. Calculations run locally in your browser.

Optical calculation

The complex Fresnel amplitudes are combined with the film’s propagation phase and attenuation. Complex Snell angles use the passive, forward-wave branch. Reflected and transmitted power use the appropriate s/p flux factors; film absorption follows from the energy balance.

Method reference: Steven J. Byrnes, Multilayer optical calculations, arXiv:1603.02720 ↗.

Model assumptions

The model assumes flat parallel boundaries, a homogeneous isotropic film, linear optical response and coherent interference. It does not represent roughness, anisotropy, finite beams, nonlinear generation or a finite substrate. A selected material dataset describes its reported sample; it is not a universal material constant.

Numerical checks

Checks include zero-thickness interfaces, s/p equality at normal incidence, Brewster-angle reflection, quarter-wave matching, absorbing-film limits and comparison against an independent transfer-matrix implementation.
